It is estimated around 8% of all adults in the United States suffer from Major Depressive Disorder (MDD). While MDD isn't the first mental illness many would think of when they think of crime, I thought it would be valuable to look into if there is any connection between MDD and violent crime, given how common it is. Is there any correlation between MDD and crime?

## What is Major Depressive Disorder?

Major Depressive Disorder, often simply referred to as depression or MDD, is a mental health disorder defined by persistent feelings of sadness and a loss of interest in activities that used to be enjoyable. MDD affects different people in different ways. Some people have mostly emotional symptoms while others have more physical symptoms, such as loss of appetite, fatigue, and increased irritability. Of course, having MDD does not inherently make someone aggressive or violent, and most people with depression don't commit crimes.

## Major Depressive Disorder in Crime

Compared to many other mental disorders, there has not been a lot of studies on the correlation between MDD and crime. I did find a [2024 Swedish population study][tayebi-link] that examined the relationship between depression and different types of criminal offenses. The study found that, similarly to my discussion about [schizophrenia][schizophrenia-post-link], a significant portion of the increased risk in individuals with depression can be attributed to the co-existence of a substance abuse disorder. They found that while individuals with MDD are about 3 times more likely to commit crimes of all types, those without a substance abuse disorder or any other mental health disorder are only about 66% more likely to commit crimes. Researchers suggest that difficulties with impulse control and emotional regulation that are attributed to individuals with MDD may explain the increase in crime rate.

Honestly, I was surprised to see that, without substance abuse being factored, MDD and schizophrenia have pretty similar effects on crime rate, with schizophrenia being about twice as likely. The media tends to portray schizophrenia in a way where you would assume it has a greater risk for violence, but this doesn't really seem to be the case. Of course, this is just one study on MDD's correlation with crime, so perhaps there's a margin of error to account for. Yet again, the real issue seems to be with substance abuse disorders that often accompany mental illness.

## Treatments and Rehabilitation

Treating MDD generally involves either antidepressant medication, psychotherapy, or a combination of both. Addressing MDD in criminals and administering treatment could help better manage their emotional state and lessen impulsivity, potentially lowering their chances of reoffending.

As with other mental disorders, treating any additional substance abuse disorder that may exist can be even more important to prevent criminal activity. [A study by Kelly and Daley][kelly-link] suggests that often, simply treating the underlying MDD with the use of antidepressant medication leads to a reduced desire to abuse substances. However, existing substance abuse can interfere with the effectiveness of antidepressant medication, due to negative effects on neurotransmission. Because of this, it is important to urge abstinence from alcohol and substances with similar effects for a period of two weeks.
